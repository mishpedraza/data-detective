# Data Detective editorial principles

Data Detective is an intellectually stimulating discovery game. Preserve nuanced questions and assume readers are capable of reasoning about the evidence. Avoid turning every question into an obvious yes-or-no choice. The best reveal surprises without manufacturing a surprising conclusion.

Accuracy is a gate. Distinguish descriptive and causal results, show the actual population and period, and identify what the chart and sources can establish. A plausible mechanism needs evidence, not a confident tone. A link or AI judgment alone cannot verify a source. Explanations should answer the specific follow-up raised by the chart and avoid generic academic summaries.

Keep the charts, with a clear link from the quiz answer to the charted evidence. Preserve the sophisticated, minimalist design. Keep answer feedback visible and entertaining. Humor should be witty, warm and compassionate, focused on the act of guessing. Correct answers should feel rewarding and incorrect answers should still be fun. Avoid unnecessary gamification and overloaded screens.

Diagnose before recommending. Describe the issue, why it matters, the specific observed evidence, the smallest improvement and how to test that improvement. Distinguish tested findings from editorial opinion. Do not propose a substantive claim change without adequate evidence.
